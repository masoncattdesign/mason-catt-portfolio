Overview

A servicing page you learn once

The Existing Card Member page is where Amazon Business card holders check their balance, pay their bill and track rewards. I led design end to end, from research through production, with PM and engineering.

The old page was rigid, hard to navigate and different on every Amazon card. The new one runs on C3 (Customer Control Center), a UX framework of widgets and templates that gives every card the same mental model, so switching cards doesn't mean relearning the page.

C3 is built on Pay Studio, the component library I shipped the year before, so teams start from tested parts instead of screenshots of production.

Why the old page wasn't working

Customer needs

Four questions

Research with 23 card members sorted their top 10 jobs into four questions: what do I owe, what are my benefits, where do I get help, and what’s happening with my account. The page is organized around them, in that order.

Customer problems

Nobody could find anything

A long learning curve, no familiar patterns, low adoption and a page that was hard to use all compounded into one problem: discoverability.

Navigation

Five menu patterns

I explored five navigation patterns. A dropdown that pushes content down won, because it matches what customers already expect from payments.

Design system

One widget, four templates

C3 is made of widgets: one building block with a fixed anatomy that adapts its content to whatever it's showing. Every card member gets a personalized page, with no one-off layouts.

  1. 1Section header. The same on every widget.
  2. 2Kebab menu. Optional quick links.
  3. 3Content area. The only part that changes between templates.
  4. 4Links. One or two actions, always at the bottom.
  5. 5Section divider. Separates widgets instead of card borders.

Decision

Dividers, not cards

Section dividers beat cards. They hold multiple items and text-heavy content, match where Amazon Shopping is heading, and give back 32px on every section.

After

What it changed

The card hub shipped in spring 2024 and is live in the Amazon Shopping app. Where C3 has shipped, NPS rose 15 points, and new features arrive as widgets instead of redesigns.

For teams, reusing C3 cut development from 6 to 8 months to 2 to 3, saved 12 engineering weeks on the baseline build and halved design approvals. It's designed to extend to more than 20 Amazon payment products, all on top of Pay Studio.
